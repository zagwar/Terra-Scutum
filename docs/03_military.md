# Terra Scutum Design: Game layers

> Part of the design doc. Index: `GAME_DESIGN.md`. Section numbers are kept across files, so "section 8.3" means the same thing everywhere.

## 5. Game Layers

### 5.1 Strategic Layer (3D world map), the core of the game
- Real-time with pause. Time advances in **days**. There are 5 speed settings, plus a pause key.
- 3D world with a top-down/angled camera that can pan, zoom and rotate. It's a **flat 3D map**, like HOI4, not a globe.
- The player picks **one real country** at the start **[DEFAULT]**. Every other country is run by AI.
- Most decisions happen here: production, research, army movement, diplomacy, influence, refugee policy and missions.

### 5.2 Operational Layer (armies on the map, HOI4-style; details in 5.5–5.7)
- Armies are **divisions** that sit in provinces. They move along borders and fight automatically.
- Moving troops into another country's province requires **military access** or an alliance.
- Units belong to one of four **domains**: land, sea, air or space (section 5.4).
- Combat resolves daily, based on attack, defense, armor, morale, supply and terrain, on laser coverage and heavy-metal disruption (section 5.4), and on the counter matrix in section 10.4.
- Players can build **fortifications** (levels 0–5) in provinces they own.

### 5.3 Unit Characterization (XCOM-style)
**There are no separate squad missions.** Every fight happens through the strategic and operational layers and can be watched in the battle view (5.7). What's kept from XCOM is the **attachment to your units**: they have names, histories, and people the player cares about losing.

#### Units have identities
- **Every division, battalion, ship and air wing has a name** (auto-generated by country style, for example "3rd Armored Division 'Iron Wolves'"), which the player can rename. It can also have an **insignia and colors** **[DEFAULT]**.
- **Service record:** battles fought, provinces defended or retaken, bugs killed by type, losses taken, medals and citations.
- **Veterancy:** experience from combat raises a unit's rank (Green → Trained → Veteran → Elite → Legendary **[DEFAULT]**), improving its stats.
- **Earned traits:** units earn traits from what they've actually done. Examples **[DEFAULT]**:
  - *Laser Hunters* (killed many Interceptors): bonus when targeting lasers
  - *Tunnel Rats* (fought many emergences): bonus against burrowers and in tunnels
  - *The Wall* (held a line at all costs): defense bonus when holding
  - *Shell-Shocked* (routed badly): morale penalty until rested
  - *Hive Breakers* (part of a hive assault)
- **Losing veterans hurts:** replacements dilute experience. A legendary unit wiped out is gone, along with its traits.

#### Named characters (the XCOM soldiers of this game)
- **Mech pilots** are the main named characters. Every mech battalion has a **commanding pilot**, and notable pilots emerge as **aces**.
  - Each named pilot has a name, nationality, rank, **experience and level**, **abilities** that unlock as they level (for example "Low-Jump Specialist," "Blade Master," "Disruption Reader"), kills by bug type, and wounds.
  - Pilots can be **wounded** (out of action for a while), **killed**, or **retire**. Aces can be moved between units, sent to train new pilots at a pilot academy, or used for propaganda (Influence and morale boosts).
  - Losing a famous ace hits public morale and the unit's morale.
- **Commanders** (generals and admirals) are named characters too. They have traits, experience and a record, and they lead armies and fleets (section 5.7).
- **Memorial:** a screen listing fallen units and characters, with their records. Like XCOM's memorial wall.
- **News and events** mention units and characters by name ("The Iron Wolves held Kharkiv for 19 days"), so the war feels personal.

#### What happened to the old squad missions
The things squad missions were meant for are now **operations** that run through the strategic layer and the Attack/Defense Planner, and can be watched in the battle view:
- **Live captures**, **hive expeditions** and **sample recovery** (section 11.1): special operations assigned to specific units (often veteran mech units with capture modules)
- **Evacuations** of civilians (section 9)
- **Tunnel raids** and **hive core assaults** (the Hive assault tactic, section 5.7)
The units that take part earn experience and traits, and their named pilots can level up or die.

### 5.4 Combined Arms (land, sea, air, space)

The war is fought in four domains, and **no single domain can win alone**. The
doctrine is modeled on Muv-Luv's war (feel only; no names or terms from it):
the Swarm's **Interceptors** (Interceptors) shoot down anything that flies, so humanity has to
rebuild warfare around that.

#### 5.4.1 Laser coverage: the bugs own the sky
- Interceptors give every province they're in, and provinces within their range, a **laser coverage** value (0–100). Range: 1 province for Interceptors, 2 for Heavy Interceptors **[DEFAULT]**.
- Laser coverage **automatically shoots at anything airborne**: aircraft, missiles, artillery shells, falling orbital units, even high-jumping mechs.
  - **Aircraft** entering a covered province take losses that scale with coverage. Above about 30 coverage, air operations are effectively suicide.
  - **Missiles, bombs and shells** are intercepted (see the interception rule below), unless the province is disrupted (5.4.2).
  - **Orbital drops and space assets** in low orbit over a covered province take heavy losses.
- **One interception rule for everything airborne.** Conventional and nuclear weapons follow the same rule. Every airborne weapon or unit entering a covered province has an **interception chance equal to its effective coverage** (after disruption and terrain):

| Intercepted (airborne / indirect fire) | Not intercepted (direct fire / ground level) |
|---|---|
| Conventional missiles (cruise, ballistic, guided) | Tank and mech guns firing directly at nearby targets |
| Bombs and air-to-ground weapons, and the aircraft carrying them | Infantry weapons, flamethrowers, mines |
| Artillery and naval shells (high-arc fire) | Ground-delivered explosives and demolition charges |
| Rockets, drones, nuclear warheads | Units moving on the ground |
| Orbital drops and orbital bombardment | Mechs staying at low jump altitude |
| Mechs that climb too high | |

  Massed fire can overwhelm a province's lasers: each Interceptor can only shoot so many targets per day, so a big enough volley gets some hits through. Every shot that gets intercepted still adds to disruption if it carries heavy metals.
- **Terrain matters:** mountains and dense cities block line of sight, which reduces effective coverage there. Open plains, deserts and sea give full coverage.
- **First contact:** Interceptors appear about 2 weeks after the first landing **[DEFAULT]**. The first time they fire, the player's air force should get mauled. That's a scripted shock moment that forces the doctrine change.
- The only way to permanently lift coverage is to **kill the Interceptors on the ground**.

#### 5.4.2 Heavy-metal disruption: blinding the lasers
- **Disruptor rounds** (artillery shells, naval shells and missiles packed with heavy-metal particles) are fired *on purpose* into laser coverage. The lasers shoot them down, and when they're destroyed they release a **heavy-metal cloud** into the province.
- Each province tracks a **disruption** value (0–100). Disruption **reduces effective laser coverage** (for example, effective coverage = coverage × (1 - disruption%)).
- Disruption **decays quickly** (it halves in about 1 day **[DEFAULT]**), and weather and terrain change how fast (section 5.4.8). It has to be sustained during an operation.
- **Heavy metals pollute.** Every disruptor round that's shot down drops toxic heavy-metal particles. Most of the cloud ends up on the ground and in the water:
  - Each province tracks **pollution** (0–100). It's added by disruption, and decays very slowly (years) unless it's cleaned up.
  - **Effects:** lower farm output, lower population growth, more health problems (happiness and stability drop), and slower refugee integration.
  - **Spread:** rain washes pollution into rivers and down to neighboring provinces, and wind carries part of the cloud. Pollution that crosses into another country is a **diplomatic issue**, with a smaller penalty than nuclear fallout.
  - **Cleanup:** a budget line and research (remediation tech) reduce it over time.
  - **The tradeoff:** heavy disruption wins battles, but poisons the land you're trying to save. Fighting over your own farmland has a long-term food cost.
- Disruptor rounds use the **heavy metals** resource (section 8.6), so a big disruption barrage is an economic cost as well.
- **The core combined-arms loop:** naval and artillery disruption barrage → lasers waste their fire on it and the cloud builds → mechs advance low under the cloud → mechs hunt and kill Interceptors → only then can air, missiles and orbital units operate freely.

#### 5.4.3 Land
**Mechs are the elite spearhead; conventional forces are the backbone.** A country can't afford an army of only mechs, and it shouldn't try.

- **Mech Walkers** are **expensive, elite** units. They **don't exist at the start**: development unlocks only after landfall (section 11.3), so the first months of the war are fought with conventional forces alone. The first mechs reaching the front is a turning point.
- They're fast and agile, armed with heavy guns and melee weapons, and fight hordes on any terrain. They move by **low-altitude jumps** that keep them under laser line of sight. Climbing too high, or doing it in an undisrupted province, gets them shot down.
  - They're designed by the player in the **Mech Designer** (section 5.4.9) and built through the **mech production chain** (mech parts factory → mech assembly plant, section 8.3).
  - Mechs need **pilots** from the separate **pilot pool** (section 8.7), trained at pilot academies, which take a long time to replace and **resupply** (ammo burns fast against hordes).
  - **Best uses:** hunting Interceptors, counterattacks, breakthroughs, hive assaults and plugging gaps in a line fast.
- **Conventional forces** (infantry, tanks, artillery, engineers, rocket artillery) are **cheap, numerous** and do most of the work:
  - **Holding defense lines:** dug-in infantry and tanks behind fortifications, minefields and walls of guns
  - **Volume of fire:** artillery and rocket batteries for disruptor barrages and normal fire support
  - **Rear security:** garrisons, guarding infrastructure, evacuation and refugee escort, putting down riots
  - **Clearing up:** taking back ground after the mechs break through
  - **Weakness:** in the open against a full horde, or when bugs emerge behind the line, conventional units get overwhelmed without mech support.
- **Division templates [DEFAULT]:** like HOI4, divisions are built from **battalions**. A division can mix a mech battalion with infantry, tanks and artillery, so a few mechs strengthen a large conventional force.
- **Artillery** is weak against laser-covered provinces with normal shells, but becomes **essential with disruptor rounds**.
- **Fortified defense lines** (fortification levels, minefields, guns) slow the hordes. Mechs counterattack through them.

#### 5.4.4 Sea
- **Battleships and gun cruisers come back.** Heavy naval guns are one of the best ways to fire disruptor rounds and supporting fire into **coastal provinces** and provinces next to them.
- **Missile ships:** strong where lasers are disrupted or absent.
- **Carriers** are mostly useless near the Swarm, because aircraft can't survive there. They're still useful for **mech carriers** (ships that carry and launch mechs for amphibious assaults) and for operations away from laser coverage.
- **Transports and amphibious landings** move divisions through sea zones and open new fronts behind Swarm lines.
- **Submarines** and escorts protect trade routes and transports.
- Navies need ports. A coastal province falling to the Swarm takes its port away.

#### 5.4.5 Air
- **Outside laser coverage**, aircraft work normally: rear-area transport, recon over safe provinces, intercepting nothing (the bugs don't fly).
- **Inside laser coverage**, aircraft are only useful in very short windows of high disruption, or after the lasers are destroyed.
- Air power becomes a **reward for winning the ground fight**: once a province's lasers are cleared, air strikes devastate the remaining hordes.
- Air units are cheap to keep in reserve but expensive to waste. The game should make the player *want* to use them and punish them for doing it too early.

#### 5.4.5.1 Air zones and basing
**Air power works from bases with a range, and is focused by operations.**
- **Bases:** air wings are stationed at an **airfield** (a building in a province), a **carrier** (in a sea zone) or an **orbital station** (space units). Each base has a **capacity** (how many wings it can hold) and needs supply (fuel, ammo, parts; section 5.5).
- **Range:** each aircraft design (section 5.4.9) has a **range measured in provinces**. A wing can operate over any province within range of its base. Moving a wing to a new base takes time.
- **Air superiority by region:** air superiority is calculated per **region** (section 4) from all the human and enemy wings that can reach it. (Against the Swarm, the "enemy" is laser coverage, section 5.4.1. Against humans, it's enemy fighters and air defense.)
- **Default missions:** by default, wings are assigned a mission over a **region** (HOI4-style): air superiority, interception, recon, close air support, strategic bombing, transport and supply drops.
- **Operation focus:** when a wing is assigned to a **Joint Operation** or a **battle plan** in the Attack/Defense Planner (section 5.7), it **focuses on that operation's provinces**, including timing its strikes to the operation's phases (only after the lasers are down, section 5.4.7).
- **Why placement matters:** airfields close to the front have the best reach but are exposed (burrowers, artillery). Airfields far back are safe but cover less. Airfields are critical infrastructure (section 5.5).

#### 5.4.6 Space (Terra Invicta + ICBM escalation)
Space combines **Terra Invicta's** orbital buildup (launch capacity, satellites, stations, the space race) with the **missile-escalation** feel of games like *ICBM: Escalation* (launches drawn as arcs over the map, interceptions, early warning, a sense that one launch can spiral).

- **Orbital layer [DEFAULT]:** space is shown as an **orbit view**, a separate screen and an optional map overlay above the flat map. Orbits are simplified into bands: **low orbit** (recon, bombardment, drops; within reach of Heavy Interceptors), **medium orbit** (navigation, communications) and **high/geostationary orbit** (early warning, communications relays). The **Moon** is in scope, but expensive (below).
- **Starting point (2025):** countries start with roughly their real 2025 space capabilities: launch sites, existing satellite networks, and a few with anti-satellite weapons.
- **Launch capacity** comes from **spaceports** (buildings) and launch vehicles. Every satellite, station module or drop pod has to be launched, and capacity is limited.
- **Satellites:**
  - **Recon:** reveals surface Swarm activity (not underground) and enemy units in human wars
  - **Communications:** a combat and coordination bonus
  - **Navigation:** improves missile and artillery accuracy
  - **Early warning:** detects missile launches (human wars), tracks the object in Phase 0, and gives weather forecasts
- **Phase 0:** satellites and telescopes track the object. A space program can attempt an **interception mission** against the object before arrival **[DEFAULT: it can reduce the number of landing pods, but can't stop the landing]**. The space race to be ready is part of the buildup.
- **Orbital stations and platforms:** modular stations built from launched modules. They host bombardment platforms, drop-pod bays and command relays.
- **Orbital bombardment:** kinetic or explosive strikes from orbit. Lasers intercept them like any other projectile, so they work best in disrupted provinces, or as a source of disruption themselves.
- **Orbital drop divisions:** elite mech units dropped from orbit straight onto a target, usually a **hive**. They take heavy losses during descent through laser coverage unless the province is heavily disrupted. This is the main way to strike a hive core deep behind Swarm lines.
- **Missile visuals (ICBM-style):** every missile launch, bombardment and drop is drawn as an **arc over the map**. Laser interceptions are shown as beams cutting the arcs down. Nuclear launches get a dramatic sequence with an alert and a global reaction.
- **The Moon (huge investment):**
  - Reaching the Moon needs advanced space techs, lunar landers (spaceship designer) and a large amount of launch capacity and money. It's a **long-term megaproject**, too big for most countries alone, which is why it's a global project.
  - **Lunar base:** built in stages (outpost, base, colony). Each stage takes years and a huge investment.
  - **Benefits [DEFAULT]:**
    - **Mining:** rare metals and other materials, shipped down to Earth
    - **Low-gravity shipyards:** cheaper construction of large spaceships and stations
    - **Research:** bonuses to space and materials science
    - **Safe from the Swarm:** it's out of laser range, and it's a backup for humanity. A lunar colony enables the **Lunar Exodus** ending if Earth is lost (section 3.3)
    - **Prestige:** a big Influence and relations boost
  - **The Moon is a global project.** **Every country and every corporation** can contribute.
    - **Shared infrastructure:** a global **Lunar Program** (run through the UN when it has authority, or by a coalition otherwise) builds shared infrastructure: landing pads, a transport route from Earth orbit, communications, and a power grid. Countries and corporations contribute money, launch capacity and goods, and get **access and priority** in proportion to what they put in.
    - **Own what you build:** every country and corporation **owns the assets it builds or sends there**: mining sites, habitats, labs, factories, shipyards, landers. Their output goes to the owner.
    - **Lunar sites [DEFAULT]:** the Moon is divided into a small number of **sites** (resource-rich craters, polar ice, the far side). Claims are settled by the UN (arbitration) or by whoever gets there first, which can create disputes.
    - **Corporations on the Moon:** corporations can build and run lunar mines and factories as private ventures, sell output to Earth, and lobby for favorable lunar rules.
- **Space in human wars:** anti-satellite weapons can destroy satellites, which blinds a country's recon, communications and early warning. Destroying satellites creates **debris** that raises the risk to every launch and satellite in that orbit band (a Kessler-style risk) **[DEFAULT]**. Shooting down satellites is a major escalation with a big diplomatic penalty.
- **Escalation ladder (human conflict):** satellite blinding, missile strikes and nuclear threats are steps on an escalation ladder. Each step raises global tension and the risk that other countries join in. Nuclear use is at the top of the ladder (section 6.1).

#### 5.4.7 Joint Operations
- The player can plan a **Joint Operation** against a target province: assign units from each domain to **phases**, which run in order:
  1. **Disruption:** naval, artillery and orbital disruptor barrages
  2. **Assault:** mechs and ground divisions attack under the cloud
  3. **Laser hunt:** mechs target Interceptors specifically
  4. **Exploitation:** air strikes and missiles once coverage is down
  5. **Hive strike** (optional): orbital drop on, or ground assault into, the hive
- The operation auto-resolves day by day, showing coverage, disruption and losses per phase. Allied countries can contribute units through **joint operations** in diplomacy (section 6).
- **Timing matters:** if disruption decays before the mechs arrive, the operation fails and the losses are heavy.

#### 5.4.8 Weather and Seasons
- Each **region** has a **weather state**, shared by all of its provinces, that changes every few days, based on its **climate** (tropical, temperate, arid, arctic, etc.) and the **season**. Seasons are reversed in the southern hemisphere.
- Weather is shown on the map, and **satellites** give a forecast a few days ahead so the player can time operations.

| Weather | Laser coverage | Disruption cloud | Other effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear | Full | Normal decay | — |
| Rain | Slightly reduced | **Washes out faster**; more pollution goes into the ground and rivers | Mud: slower ground movement and supply |
| Storm | Reduced | Disperses very fast | No air or orbital operations; naval operations limited |
| Strong wind | Full | Blown into downwind provinces (spreading pollution there) | — |
| Fog | **Reduced** (the beam is scattered) | Lasts longer | Lower recon; ambush risk |
| Dust / sandstorm | **Reduced** | Lasts longer | Slower movement; equipment wear |
| Snow / winter | Full | Normal | Much slower movement and supply; higher upkeep; attrition for unprepared units |

- **Seasons** also affect **food**: farms produce most in the growing season and little in winter, so food reserves matter.
- **The Swarm** is affected too: cold slows bug movement, heat speeds it up **[DEFAULT]**.
- **Using weather:** launching an assault under fog or a dust storm weakens the lasers. Waiting out a storm before an orbital drop saves lives. Rain makes disruption cost more but pollute differently.

#### 5.4.9 Designers (HOI4-style)
There are **six designers**, all using the same system: a **base** (chassis, hull or frame) plus **modules** in slots, all unlocked by research. Designs are saved, produced through production chains, and can be refitted. Each design gets a name the player can change.

| Designer | Base | Example module slots |
|---|---|---|
| **Mech** | Frame (Gen 1/2/3, heavy, light, command) | See the mech section below |
| **Tank / vehicle** | Chassis (light, medium, heavy, IFV, self-propelled gun) | Main gun, turret, armor, engine, suspension, special (flamethrower, mine plow, anti-air) |
| **Aircraft** | Airframe (fighter, attack, bomber, transport, drone) | Weapons, engine, armor, avionics/ECM, fuel tanks, special (disruptor dispenser) |
| **Ship** | Hull (destroyer, cruiser, battleship, carrier, mech carrier, submarine, transport) | Main battery, secondary guns, missiles, armor, engine, radar/sonar, air defense, special |
| **Spaceship** | Hull (launch shuttle, drop ship, orbital fighter, transport, lunar lander) | Engine, fuel, cargo/drop bays, armor, weapons, sensors |
| **Space station** | Core module (small, medium, large) | Habitat, bombardment platform, drop-pod bay, sensors, command relay, shipyard, lab, power, defense |

- Each design shows its stats, cost (goods from the production chains), production time and which **tech variant** (anti-Swarm or anti-human, section 11.2) its modules use.
- **Mech designer details:**
- Players **design their own mechs**, the same way HOI4's tank designer works.
- A design = a **frame** (chassis) + **modules** in slots. Frames and modules are unlocked through the research tree (section 11).
- **Frames** set the generation, the number and type of slots, base armor, mobility, jump height and cost. **[DEFAULT]** examples:
  - Gen 1: heavy and slow; low jumps only; a few slots
  - Gen 2: lighter, faster; more slots
  - Gen 3: very agile; high jump control; many slots; expensive
  - Variants: a heavy frame for assault, a light frame for recon, a command frame
- **Module slots:**

| Slot | Example modules |
|---|---|
| Main weapon | Assault cannon, autocannon, heavy rifle, support cannon |
| Secondary / melee | Combat blade, close-combat knife, shield |
| Back mount | Missile pods, extra weapon, supply pack, disruptor launcher |
| Armor | Light composite, heavy plate, acid-resistant coating, laser-resistant coating |
| Mobility | Jump thrusters, improved actuators, all-terrain legs |
| Electronics | Sensors (detect burrowers), communications (combat bonus), data link |
| Special | Orbital-drop shell, amphibious kit, demolition charge (for hive cores or nuclear devices) |

- **Stats a design calculates:** attack (soft vs horde, hard vs armored bugs), defense, armor, speed, **jump safety** (how exposed it is to lasers), reliability, ammo use, build cost (industry + rare metals), production time, and **pilot skill needed**.
- **Saved designs** go into production at **mech assembly plants**, and each module adds its own parts to the recipe. **Refitting** existing mechs to a new design costs less than building new ones.
- **Design tradeoffs:** heavier armor means a slower mech that's more exposed when jumping; more weapons mean it runs out of ammo faster; laser coatings are expensive.

### 5.5 Supply and Logistics
**Supply lines are one of the most important systems in the game.** An army that runs out of supplies can't fight back effectively, however strong it is on paper.

#### Supply types
| Supply | Used by | Running out means |
|---|---|---|
| **Ammunition** | Every combat unit; mechs and artillery use a lot; disruptor rounds are a separate type | Attack and defense collapse; units can barely fight back |
| **Fuel** | Tanks, mechs, trucks, ships, aircraft, generators | Units can't move or retreat; mechs can't jump; aircraft and ships stay put |
| **Rations** | Every unit (people have to eat) | Morale and organization drain every day; attrition losses |
| **Spare parts [DEFAULT]** | Mechs, vehicles, aircraft | Breakdowns; fewer units ready to fight |
| **Electricity** | Depots, field hospitals, mech maintenance bays, factories, cities | Mech repair and recharging stop; depots work slower; blackouts (below) |

#### The supply chain
1. **Production:** factories produce ammo, fuel, parts and processed food, which go into the **national stockpile** (or are bought from corporations in market economies, section 8.13).
2. **Transport:** supplies move from the stockpile to the front over **rail** (high capacity), **roads/trucks** (flexible, lower capacity, uses fuel) and **ports and sea zones** (for overseas fronts). Each link has a **throughput** per day.
3. **Supply depots:** buildings near the front that **stockpile** supplies (with a storage limit). Units draw from the nearest depot connected to them.
4. **Units:** each unit draws what it needs every day. Combat multiplies ammo use; movement multiplies fuel use.

- **Depots need restocking.** A depot holds a buffer, so the front keeps fighting for a while if the line is cut, but it drains steadily and has to be refilled through the supply network.
- **Throughput limits:** a front can only be supplied as fast as its rail, road and port capacity allows. A big offensive needs logistics to be built up first (more rail, depots, ports).
- **Encirclement:** units cut off from all depots live on what they carry, then collapse.
- **Weather** (mud, snow, storms) and **refugee traffic** reduce throughput (sections 5.4.8 and 9).

#### Critical infrastructure
Some buildings keep everything else running. **Defending them matters as much as holding the line.**

| Infrastructure | If it's damaged or destroyed |
|---|---|
| **Power plant** | Less electricity for the whole grid; rolling blackouts if demand exceeds supply |
| **Electrical substation** | **Blackout** in the provinces it serves until it's repaired |
| **Rail hub / junction** | Rail throughput through it drops to near zero; supply has to reroute |
| **Port** | Ships can't be supplied, repaired or unloaded there |
| **Fuel depot / refinery** | Fuel shortages for everything that depends on it |
| **Supply depot** | The front units it feeds start draining their own reserves |

- **Blackouts:** a blacked-out province has factories stopped, depots working slowly, no mech repair, a big **stability drop**, and food spoiling. It lasts **until the building is repaired** using construction capacity.
- **Rolling blackouts:** when the grid has less power than demand, the player sets **priorities** (military industry, supply network, civilians, research). The lowest priority gets cut first.
- **Threats:** Swarm units emerging behind the lines (burrowers) target infrastructure. Fighting in a province damages it. In human wars, sabotage, air strikes and missiles target it.
- **Protection:** garrisons, fortifications, air defense (against humans), seismic sensors (against burrowers) and backup infrastructure (extra substations, redundant rail lines).

### 5.6 Combat Model (HOI4 + Total War)
**The math is HOI4-style; the feel is Total War.** Battles are fought by divisions in provinces and resolved by the simulation, using HOI4-style stats plus Total War-style morale, flanking and fatigue.

- **Unit stats (HOI4-style):** strength (HP), **organization** (how long the unit keeps fighting), soft attack (against hordes and infantry), hard attack (against armored targets), defense, breakthrough, armor and piercing, **frontage** (how much room it takes in a battle), speed, and supply use.
- **Combat width:** each battle has a width set by terrain. Only that many units fight at once; the rest wait as **reserves** and rotate in.
- **Damage:** each combat round (every few hours of game time), units roll attacks against enemy units in the battle. Damage depends on attack vs defense or breakthrough, armor vs piercing, terrain, weather, supply, laser coverage (section 5.4), the counter matrix (10.4) and modifiers.
- **Total War elements:**
  - **Morale and routing:** units that lose too much organization or morale **rout**. They retreat in disorder, and other units nearby lose morale. Bug hordes don't rout, but **hive-link disruption [DEFAULT]** (killing nearby Swarm structures) can make them falter.
  - **Flanking:** attacking a province from several sides gives big bonuses; being surrounded hurts morale.
  - **Fatigue:** units fighting for days lose effectiveness until rotated out.
  - **Unit experience:** veteran units fight better and hold longer.
  - **Commander traits [DEFAULT, see section 17]:** generals affect morale, flanking and supply use.
- **Swarm combat:** bugs come in huge numbers with low organization but no morale loss. They win by mass and by attacking from tunnels behind the line.
- **Results** update each unit's strength, organization, morale, supplies and experience, plus the province's buildings (damage) and control.

### 5.7 Army Control and Battle View
#### Army control (HOI4-style)
- Divisions are grouped into **armies** under commanders.
- The player draws **front lines** along borders or Swarm fronts and assigns armies to them. Units automatically spread out and defend the line.
- **Battle plans:** draw an **offensive arrow** to a target, and the army attacks along it when ordered. Set **fallback lines** to retreat to automatically.
- Players can still **move individual divisions by hand** at any time.
- **Joint Operations** (section 5.4.7) layer naval, air, orbital and disruption support on top of battle plans.
- **Automation [DEFAULT]:** the AI handles unit placement along a front; the player decides the plan.

#### Attack/Defense Planner
**The player fights battles by planning them, not by controlling units directly.** Every battle plan (offensive arrow), front line and Joint Operation has a plan that sets **how units behave, which tactics they use, and what support is committed**. The plan can be changed at any time, including while watching a battle, but changes take a few hours of game time to reach the troops **[DEFAULT]**.

**Unit behavior (per army, or per division as an override):**
| Behavior | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Aggressive** | Attacks harder and pursues retreating enemies; takes more losses and uses more ammo |
| **Balanced** | Default |
| **Cautious** | Fewer losses; slower advance; retreats earlier |
| **Hold at all costs** | Never retreats; fights to the last; huge morale strain |
| **Fighting retreat** | Gives ground slowly to the fallback line, inflicting losses while withdrawing |

**Tactics (choose one per plan; each counters some Swarm or human tactics and is weak to others):**
| Offensive tactics | | Defensive tactics | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frontal assault** | Simple, heavy losses, works with overwhelming firepower | **Hold the line** | Static defense; strong with fortifications; weak to tunnel attacks |
| **Envelopment / flanking** | Attack from several provinces; big bonus if it works; needs mobile units | **Defense in depth** | Several layered lines; absorbs breakthroughs; good against hordes |
| **Mech infiltration** | Mechs jump past the front under the disruption cloud to hit Interceptors and Swarm structures | **Elastic defense** | Fall back, then counterattack; good against hordes that overextend; needs mechs or armor |
| **Siege / attrition** | Steady artillery and naval bombardment before advancing; slow, low losses, uses lots of ammo | **Kill zones** | Funnel hordes into prepared fire zones; needs engineers, mines and artillery |
| **Hive assault** | Special tactic for hive cores: tunnel-clearing teams plus orbital drop | **Tunnel watch** | Units held back to react to burrower emergences behind the line; needs seismic sensors |

**Priorities and rules:**
- **Target priority:** Interceptors first, nearest threats, Swarm structures, or human units by type (in human wars)
- **Protect:** critical infrastructure, cities or specific provinces (section 5.5)
- **Retreat threshold:** at what strength or organization units fall back
- **Ammo use:** conserve (lower damage, supplies last longer), normal, or maximum fire
- **Rules of engagement:** for example, whether artillery can fire into your own populated provinces (civilian casualties and pollution)

**Support commitment (pick what supports this plan, and how much):**
- **Artillery and rocket artillery:** normal fire and/or **disruptor barrages**
- **Naval gunfire** from adjacent sea zones
- **Air support:** only when laser coverage is low or cleared (section 5.4.5)
- **Orbital support:** bombardment and drops
- **Reserves:** which divisions wait to be committed, and when (automatic at a threshold, or on the player's order)
- **Engineers:** fortifications, mines, bridges, tunnel demolition
- **Supply priority:** which plan gets supplies first when there isn't enough for everything

- **Joint Operations** (section 5.4.7) are the large, multi-phase version of an attack plan. They use the same planner, with phases added.
- **The AI** (other countries, and the player's own armies on automation) uses the same planner.
- **The Swarm has tactics too [DEFAULT]:** horde rush, tunnel ambush, laser screen, siege beasts leading the attack. Hive knowledge (section 11.1) lets the player predict which one is coming.
- **Templates:** players can save plans ("Standard anti-horde defense") and reuse them.

#### Battle view (watch the fight, Total War-style)
- **Zooming in** on a province with a battle shows the fight in 3D: mechs jumping, tanks firing, artillery arcs, laser beams cutting down shells, the heavy-metal cloud, bug hordes pouring out of tunnels.
- The simulation decides the result; the battle view **shows** it. What you see matches the numbers: losses, routing, laser interceptions, supply running low (units firing less).
- **The battle view is watch-only.** The player controls the camera and time speed, and can open the **Attack/Defense Planner** (below) to change the plan. There's no micro-control of individual units. **How a battle goes is decided by the plan the player set up.**
- **The world map also shows units:** 3D unit models stand in their provinces and animate when they're in combat, like HOI4's map units but more alive.
- **Performance:** battles can involve thousands of bugs. Use instancing (thin instances), level of detail, and only fully render the battle being watched (section 14).
